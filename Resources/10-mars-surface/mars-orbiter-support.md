# What an orbiter gives a surface team

> Webs: PDS Imaging Node, JPL and NASA Science pages listed per row. Accessed 2026-11-15.
> Data: rows `O01` to `O07` of [`data/mars-surface-numbers.csv`](data/mars-surface-numbers.csv)

## Maps and weather (Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter)
- PDS Imaging Node mission page: HiRISE "ground sampling dimension between 25 and 32 cm/pixel"; CTX "acquiring grayscale (black & white) images at 6 meters per pixel scale over a swath 30 kilometers wide"; MARCI "observing the entire planet every day at 5 visible and 2 ultraviolet wavelengths". MCS is only listed by name there. **[VERIFICADO-NASA]**
- NASA Science, MRO page: launched "Aug. 12, 2005", "serving as the first link in a communications bridge back to Earth", active.
- JPL 2009: the camera "sees the entire planet every day at resolution comparable to weather satellites around Earth"; observations of atmospheric change let rover teams know when dust is heading their way.
- JPL 2012-11-21: a storm was first observed by the Mars Color Imager on 10 November and reported to the Opportunity team; on 16 November the Mars Climate Sounder "detected a warming of the atmosphere". "If the storm were to go global, the Opportunity rover would be affected most." The page does not give a warning lead time.

Player: HiRISE shows rocks and slopes about 25 to 32 cm across for route planning; MARCI gives a daily picture of dust. The orbiter also carries the rover's data (see [`mars-communications.md`](mars-communications.md)).

## Storm forecasting practice
Practice seen in NASA pages: watch the daily global map, confirm with temperature profiles, warn the rover team, cut activity and conserve energy (2009 and 2012 pages above). The 2018 case: Opportunity went silent on 2018-06-10; Curiosity, on an RTG, "expected to remain largely unaffected". **[VERIFICADO-NASA]**
