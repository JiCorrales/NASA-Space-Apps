# Mars temperature at rover sites

> Webs: JPL and NASA Science pages listed per row. Accessed 2026-11-15.
> Data: rows `T01` to `T15` of [`data/mars-surface-numbers.csv`](data/mars-surface-numbers.csv)

## Curiosity, Gale Crater (REMS)
JPL, "Second Cycle of Martian Seasons Completing for Curiosity Rover" (2016-05-11): REMS "has measured air temperatures from 60.5 degrees Fahrenheit (15.9 degrees Celsius) on a summer afternoon" "to minus 148 F (minus 100 C) on a winter night". Nights "plummet overnight below minus 130 F (minus 90 C)". Gale is "clear in winter, dustier in spring and summer, and windy in autumn". NASA Science, "Seasonal Cycles in Curiosity's First Two Martian Years": "the air temperature around Curiosity usually plummets by more than 100 Fahrenheit degrees". **[VERIFICADO-NASA]**, period: first two Mars years (to May 2016).

Player: a sol at Gale runs from just above freezing to below -90 C. Heaters and the RTG waste heat matter more than the daytime peak.

## Perseverance, Jezero Crater (MEDA)
- NASA Science, "Daily Records of Atmospheric Temperature With Perseverance" (2023-08-01): "surface temperature typically ranges from -83 Celsius pre-dawn to 5 Celsius at noon (between -83 Celsius and -23 Celsius for air temperature)". The page speaks of Mars in general, not only Jezero **[VERIFICADO-NASA]**.
- Mean -55 C and day-night differences of 50 to 60 C: from a university press release on a Nature Geoscience paper (UPV/EHU, 2023-01-18): "the average air temperature at the Jezero Crater ... is around minus 55 degrees Celsius", "typical differences of around 50 to 60 degrees". **[FUENTE-NO-NASA]**: the paper is peer reviewed and uses NASA's rover, but NASA has no page with these numbers. The paper itself could not be opened (login redirect).

## InSight, Elysium Planitia
JPL, "InSight Is the Newest Mars Weather Service" (2019-02-19): one late-winter day, "a high of 2 degrees Fahrenheit (-17 degrees Celsius)", "low of -138 degrees Fahrenheit (-95 degrees Celsius)", top wind 37.8 mph (16.9 m/s). **[VERIFICADO-NASA]** but a single sol, not a seasonal range. Day-night difference about 78 C **[DERIVADO]**.

## Other NASA figures
- Fact Sheet: average about 214 K (-59 C); diurnal range at the Viking 1 site 184 to 242 K (-89 to -31 C). Viking is from 1976.
- NASA Science, "Mars: Facts": "as high as 70 degrees Fahrenheit (20 degrees Celsius)" and "as low as about -225 degrees Fahrenheit (-153 degrees Celsius)". The cold end is a polar-night value, not a rover-site one.
